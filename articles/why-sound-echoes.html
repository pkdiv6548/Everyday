SOUND

Why do you hear an echo in some places?

Sound waves bounce off hard surfaces and return to your ears.

4 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerShout in a large empty hall or canyon and you may hear your voice again. The returning sound is an echo: your sound wave travelled to a surface and reflected back.

The sound needs time to return

Sound travels through air and reflects from hard surfaces such as rock, concrete or a wall. If the reflected sound reaches you after a noticeable delay—often around a tenth of a second or more—you may hear it as a separate echo. Nearby surfaces usually blend the reflection into the original sound as reverberation.

Why do curtains reduce echoes?

Soft, porous materials can absorb some sound energy instead of reflecting all of it. Curtains, carpets and padded furniture often reduce sharp reflections in a room. Large, hard, bare surfaces tend to send more sound back.

Try a safe mini experiment

Clap once in a furnished room and then in a safe, empty hallway. Compare how long the sound seems to linger. Do not shout close to anyone's ears.

Words made simpleReflection is a wave bouncing off a surface. Reverberation is many quick reflections blending together.
