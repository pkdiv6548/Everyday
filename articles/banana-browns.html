Why does a cut banana turn brown?

Air starts a tiny chemical change on the fruit’s surface.

4 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerCut a banana and leave it for a few minutes. The pale surface slowly turns brown. It is not dirt appearing from nowhere; the fruit is reacting with oxygen in the air.

The fruit’s built-in chemistry

When fruit cells are cut or bruised, an enzyme inside them can meet oxygen from the air. This helps create brown-coloured substances on the exposed surface. The process is called enzymatic browning.

Can you slow it down?

A little lemon juice can slow browning because it makes the surface more acidic. Covering the cut fruit also reduces how much air reaches it. Browning does not automatically mean the fruit is unsafe, but use normal freshness and food-safety checks.

Try a safe mini experiment

Try this with an adult: put equal banana slices on two plates. Add a few drops of lemon juice to one slice and leave the other plain. Compare them after 15–20 minutes. Do not eat fruit that smells or looks spoiled.

Words made simpleA good experiment changes one thing at a time, observes carefully and compares results. Never taste unknown materials or use dangerous heat, chemicals or electricity.
