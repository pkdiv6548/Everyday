PLANT SCIENCE

Why do leaves change colour in autumn?

As days shorten, green chlorophyll breaks down and other pigments show through.

5 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerMany leaves are green in warm months because they contain chlorophyll. When daylight and temperature change, some trees stop replacing that green pigment, revealing yellow and orange pigments already in the leaf.

Green is not the only pigment

Chlorophyll helps plants capture light for photosynthesis. Leaves can also contain yellow-orange carotenoids, which are often hidden by the stronger green. In some species, red-purple anthocyanins are produced in autumn and can create bright red shades.

Why do some trees stay green?

Evergreen trees have leaves or needles adapted to last through colder seasons. Their shape and protective coatings help them conserve water and tolerate harsh conditions. Different species respond differently to local climate, so autumn colour varies from place to place.

Try a safe mini experiment

Collect only fallen leaves from different trees, compare their colours and shapes, and sketch what you notice. Do not pull leaves from living plants or taste any plant material.

Words made simplePigments are substances that give colour. Chlorophyll is the green pigment that helps capture light.
