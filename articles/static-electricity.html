Why does a balloon stick to a wall?

Invisible electric charges can pull on ordinary objects.

4 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerRub a balloon on dry hair and bring it close to a wall. Sometimes it sticks for a little while. No glue is involved: the trick is static electricity.

A tiny transfer of charge

When two different materials rub together, tiny charged particles called electrons can move from one surface to the other. The balloon then has an electric charge. This is called static electricity because the charge stays in one place for a while.

Why can a neutral wall attract it?

The balloon’s charge slightly shifts charges inside the wall’s surface. The nearer opposite charge is pulled more strongly than the farther like charge is pushed away, so the balloon can stick temporarily. Humid air often makes the effect fade faster.

Try a safe mini experiment

Try it: rub an inflated balloon on dry hair for 10–15 seconds, then gently bring it near small paper pieces or a clean wall. Keep it away from electrical sockets, electronics and anyone who dislikes static shocks.

Words made simpleA good experiment changes one thing at a time, observes carefully and compares results. Never taste unknown materials or use dangerous heat, chemicals or electricity.
