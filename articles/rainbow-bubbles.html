LIGHT & COLOUR

Why do soap bubbles show rainbow colours?

A film thinner than a hair can split reflected light into colours.

4 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerA soap bubble looks like a tiny rainbow globe. The colours are not painted onto it; they appear because light reflects from the bubble's front and back surfaces.

Two reflections meet

A bubble is a very thin layer of soapy water with air on both sides. Some light reflects from the outside surface and some from the inside surface. The reflected waves overlap. For some colours they strengthen each other; for others they partly cancel. This is called thin-film interference.

Why do the colours move?

The film is constantly draining and changing thickness. As its thickness changes, different colours reinforce one another in different places. When the film becomes extremely thin, a patch can look dark just before the bubble bursts.

Try a safe mini experiment

Blow bubbles outdoors in daylight and look at them against a dark background. Gently tilt your view and notice how the colours shift. Avoid soap solution in your eyes.

Words made simpleInterference happens when light waves overlap. A thin film is a very narrow layer of liquid.
