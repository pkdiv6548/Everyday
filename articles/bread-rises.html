What makes bread dough rise?

Yeast releases gas that gets trapped in stretchy dough.

5 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerBread dough can grow much bigger while it rests. Tiny living yeast cells use sugars and release carbon dioxide gas, which becomes trapped in the dough and creates bubbles.

Tiny bubbles stretch the dough

Yeast is a microorganism. In warm, moist dough, it can use available sugars and produce carbon dioxide. Gluten proteins in wheat dough form a stretchy network that holds many gas bubbles instead of letting them escape immediately.

Why does baking change the texture?

In the oven, gas expands and more vapour forms. The dough's structure then sets as proteins change and starch thickens. Too little time may leave dense bread; too much rising can weaken the bubble network and make it collapse.

Try a safe mini experiment

With an adult, watch dough before and after resting using a clear bowl. Mark its starting height on the outside and compare later. Follow a trusted recipe; do not eat raw dough made with uncooked flour.
