LIGHT & COLOUR

Why is the sky blue, but sunsets are orange?

The colours above us are a daily physics demonstration. The secret is how air scatters different wavelengths of sunlight.

5 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES

Original illustration by Everyday Oddities

The short answerThe colours above us are a daily physics demonstration. The secret is how air scatters different wavelengths of sunlight.

A beam of many colours

Sunlight looks white, but it contains a spectrum of colours. As it enters the atmosphere, tiny gas molecules scatter shorter blue wavelengths more effectively than longer red wavelengths. This is called Rayleigh scattering.

Why sunsets turn warm

At sunset, sunlight travels through more atmosphere before reaching your eyes. Much of the blue light gets scattered out of the direct path, leaving more reds and oranges to reach you.

Look for it yourself

Compare the sky around midday with the sky near sunset on a clear day. Haze, clouds and air pollution can change the effect, so the colours will not be identical every day.
