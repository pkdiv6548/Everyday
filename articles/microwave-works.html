What actually happens inside a microwave?

A microwave oven doesn't heat food from the inside out by magic. Electromagnetic waves transfer energy to molecules in food, especially polar molecules such as water.

4 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerA microwave oven doesn't heat food from the inside out by magic. Electromagnetic waves transfer energy to molecules in food, especially polar molecules such as water.

Waves meet molecules

The oven produces microwaves that create changing electric fields. Molecules with an uneven distribution of charge respond to those fields, and energy is transferred into the food. The detailed heating process is more complex than simply making water molecules spin.

Why some spots get hotter

The wave pattern inside the oven can create hot and cool spots. A turntable helps move food through those regions, while stirring and resting help heat spread more evenly.

Use it safely

Use containers labelled microwave-safe, follow the appliance instructions, and be careful with steam and hot liquids. Never run an empty microwave unless its manual explicitly permits it.
