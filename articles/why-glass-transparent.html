Why can we see through glass?

Visible light passes through ordinary clear glass with relatively little absorption.

4 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerVisible light passes through ordinary clear glass with relatively little absorption.

What is happening?

The electronic structure of glass means visible-light photons are not strongly absorbed by the material. Much of the light passes through, though some is reflected at each surface.

Why does it matter?

Tinted or frosted glass scatters or absorbs more light, reducing the clear image that reaches your eyes.
