Why do snowflakes often have six sides?

Water molecules form a six-sided crystal structure as ice grows.

4 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerWater molecules form a six-sided crystal structure as ice grows.

What is happening?

In ordinary atmospheric ice, molecules form a hexagonal crystal lattice. This molecular geometry creates the six-fold symmetry common in snow crystals.

Why does it matter?

Temperature and humidity shape each branch, so individual snowflakes develop different patterns.
