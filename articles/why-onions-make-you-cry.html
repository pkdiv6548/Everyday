Why do onions make your eyes water?

Cutting an onion releases compounds that can irritate your eyes.

5 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerAn onion is not trying to make you cry. When its cells are cut, chemicals that were stored separately mix and form a gas that reaches your eyes.

What happens when the onion is cut?

Inside an intact onion, different ingredients are kept in separate compartments. A knife breaks the cells, allowing an enzyme to help form a volatile, sulphur-containing compound. It travels through the air and dissolves in the moisture on your eyes, causing irritation.

How can you reduce the sting?

A sharp knife crushes fewer cells than a blunt one. Chilling an onion briefly may slow the reaction. Cutting near good ventilation can carry the gas away. These tricks can help, but they do not guarantee tear-free chopping.

Try a safe mini experiment

With an adult, compare how quickly the smell reaches you when an onion is cut in a ventilated area versus a still room. Do not put onion near your eyes or taste anything during the test.

Words made simpleVolatile means a substance can easily enter the air as a gas. Enzymes help chemical reactions happen faster.
