Why do fingers wrinkle in water?

The familiar bath-time wrinkles are not simply water soaking into skin.

5 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerAfter a long bath, the skin on your fingertips can look wrinkled. It seems like the skin has soaked up water like a sponge, but nerves in your fingers play an important role.

Your nerves help make the wrinkles

In warm water, blood vessels beneath the skin narrow in a controlled way, changing the shape of the skin above them. This response is linked to the autonomic nervous system—the part that controls body functions you do not have to think about.

Could wrinkles help with grip?

One idea is that the grooves may help fingers grip wet objects, much like tyre treads help move water away. Scientists have studied this idea, but the full reason and usefulness are still being researched. The skin usually returns to normal after drying.

Try a safe mini experiment

Try a gentle observation: look at your fingertips before and after a normal bath. Do not stay in hot water for extra time just to make wrinkles. If skin pain or unusual changes persist, ask a trusted adult or health professional.

Words made simpleA good experiment changes one thing at a time, observes carefully and compares results. Never taste unknown materials or use dangerous heat, chemicals or electricity.
