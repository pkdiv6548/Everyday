SOUND & WEATHER

Why do we see lightning before hearing thunder?

Light reaches you much faster than sound travels through air.

4 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerLightning and thunder happen in the same storm event, but your eyes and ears receive them at different times because light travels far faster than sound.

A huge difference in speed

Light travels at about 300,000 kilometres per second, while sound in ordinary air travels at roughly 343 metres per second near room temperature. Over a few kilometres, the light arrives almost instantly, while the sound takes several seconds.

Can the delay estimate distance?

If you safely hear thunder after a flash, count the seconds between them. Roughly every three seconds corresponds to about one kilometre. This is only a rough estimate; if thunder is audible, the storm may already be dangerously close.

Try a safe mini experiment

During a storm, observe only from inside a substantial building or a closed vehicle. Never stand outdoors, under a tree or near water to measure the delay. Go indoors as soon as thunder is heard.

Words made simpleA second is a unit of time. Sound needs a material such as air to travel through; light does not need air.
