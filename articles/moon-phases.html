Why does the Moon change shape each night?

We see different portions of the Moon's sunlit half as it orbits Earth.

5 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerThe Moon does not actually shrink and grow every month. The Sun always lights up roughly half of it, but as the Moon travels around Earth, we see different portions of that bright half.

Sunlight makes the phases

The Moon is not a lamp; it reflects sunlight. When it is between Earth and the Sun, the side facing us is mostly dark and we see a new Moon. As it moves along its orbit, more of the sunlit side becomes visible, then less again. The full cycle takes about 29.5 days.

A safe model you can make

Hold a ball at arm's length in a room and use a lamp as the Sun. Slowly turn your body while keeping the ball in view. The lit portion you see changes, giving you a model of lunar phases. Never look directly into a bright lamp or the Sun.

Try a safe mini experiment

Try the ball-and-lamp model with an adult. Draw the shape of the bright part from four positions and compare your sketches.

Words made simpleAn orbit is the path one object follows around another. A phase is the appearance of the Moon's lit portion from Earth.
