The strange reason ice floats

Most solids are denser than their liquids. Water is an unusual exception: when it freezes, its molecules form an open crystal structure that takes up more space.

6 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerMost solids are denser than their liquids. Water is an unusual exception: when it freezes, its molecules form an open crystal structure that takes up more space.

A crystal with extra space

In liquid water, hydrogen bonds constantly form and break. As water freezes, the molecules settle into a more ordered structure that leaves gaps. The same amount of water therefore occupies more volume as ice.

Density makes it float

Because ice has lower density than liquid water, buoyancy pushes it upward. Ice floating on lakes also forms an insulating layer, helping the water below remain liquid during cold weather.

A small quirk, a big impact

This behaviour helps aquatic ecosystems survive winter. It is one reason water's molecular structure matters far beyond the freezer tray.
