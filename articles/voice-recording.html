SOUND

Why does your recorded voice sound strange?

You hear your own voice through air and through vibrations inside your head.

4 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerMany people dislike hearing their recorded voice at first. The recording is not necessarily wrong: you normally hear your voice through two routes, but a microphone captures mainly the sound travelling through air.

Two routes to your ears

When you speak, sound travels through the air to your ears. Vibrations also travel through tissues and bones in your head. This bone conduction changes the mix of frequencies you hear, often making your own voice seem deeper or fuller to you than it sounds on a recording.

Can you get used to it?

Yes. Listening to short recordings over time can make the sound feel more familiar. Microphone distance, room echo and the device can also change the result, so a phone recording is not a perfect measure of how everyone else hears you.

Try a safe mini experiment

Record one short sentence in a quiet room, then listen at a comfortable volume. Try a second recording from a different distance and notice how the sound changes. Ask permission before recording anyone else.

Words made simpleFrequency describes how quickly a sound wave vibrates. Bone conduction means vibrations reach the inner ear through the bones and tissues.
