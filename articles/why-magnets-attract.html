MAGNETISM

Why does a magnet attract some metals but not others?

The tiny magnetic structure inside a material determines how it responds.

5 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerA magnet can pick up an iron nail but not a copper coin. The reason is not simply that one is metal and the other is not: different metals have different internal magnetic properties.

Tiny magnetic regions

Iron, nickel and cobalt can be strongly attracted to ordinary magnets because their magnetic moments can line up in regions called domains. In an unmagnetized piece, many domains point in different directions. A nearby magnet can help align them, creating a strong attraction.

Why not copper or aluminium?

Copper and aluminium respond differently to a normal permanent magnet; they are not strongly pulled like iron. Aluminium can respond to changing magnetic fields in special situations, but that is different from the familiar attraction of a steel paper clip. Not every metal behaves alike.

Try a safe mini experiment

Try a fridge magnet with an adult and compare a steel paper clip, aluminium foil, copper-coloured coin and wooden pencil. Keep magnets away from electronics, cards and medical devices.

Words made simpleA domain is a region where many tiny magnetic moments point in a similar direction. Attraction is a pulling force.
