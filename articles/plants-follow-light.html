PLANT SCIENCE

Why do plants bend toward a window?

A plant's growing parts respond to the direction of light.

5 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerPlace a potted plant near a bright window and, over time, its stem may lean toward the light. This is not the plant making a conscious choice; growth itself responds to light direction.

Growth can be uneven

In many shoots, the plant hormone auxin helps control cell growth. Light changes how auxin is distributed, so cells on the shaded side can lengthen more than cells on the bright side. That difference makes the stem curve toward the light. The response is called phototropism.

Can you observe it at home?

A plant needs enough light to make food through photosynthesis. Turning a pot occasionally can help growth stay more balanced, but each plant has different needs. Do not keep a plant in darkness for a long test; it may become weak.

Try a safe mini experiment

Take a photo of a plant near a window today, then observe it from the same angle over several days. Keep watering and care the same, and note the direction of new growth.

Words made simplePhototropism is growth in response to light. Photosynthesis is the process plants use to make sugars from light, water and carbon dioxide.
