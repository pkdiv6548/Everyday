Why do chillies feel hot?

Capsaicin activates heat-sensitive receptors in the mouth.

4 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerCapsaicin activates heat-sensitive receptors in the mouth.

What is happening?

Capsaicin in chilli peppers binds to TRPV1 receptors, which normally respond to heat. The brain interprets that signal as burning even though the tissue is not actually being burned.

Why does it matter?

Capsaicin dissolves better in fats than in water, so milk or yoghurt may relieve the sensation better than plain water for many people.
