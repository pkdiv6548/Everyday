Why do stars twinkle but planets usually do not?

Moving layers of air bend starlight in constantly changing ways.

4 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerStars are so far away that they look like tiny points of light. Earth's moving atmosphere bends their light slightly from moment to moment, making them seem to flicker.

Air is never perfectly still

Air layers differ in temperature and density. As starlight passes through them, its path changes a little. The star's apparent brightness and position can wobble rapidly; astronomers call this atmospheric scintillation.

Why are planets steadier?

Planets are much closer and appear as tiny discs rather than perfect points. Light from different parts of the disc is distorted in slightly different ways, which averages out some of the flicker. Planets can still twinkle when the air is very turbulent.

Try a safe mini experiment

On a clear night, compare a bright star and a bright planet if you can identify them using a reliable sky guide. Observe from a safe place and never stare at the Sun.

Words made simpleAtmosphere is the layer of gases around Earth. Density tells how much material is packed into a given space.
