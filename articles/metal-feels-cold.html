Why does metal feel colder than wood?

The two objects may be the same temperature—but move heat differently.

4 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerOn a cool day, touch a metal spoon and a wooden spoon kept in the same room. The metal often feels colder. Usually, both have been sitting at almost the same room temperature.

Your hand is the heat source

Your hand is warmer than the spoons, so heat flows from your skin into each spoon. Metal carries heat away from your skin quickly. Wood is a poor heat conductor, so it takes heat away more slowly.

A fair test

Leave a metal spoon and a wooden spoon in the same room for at least an hour. Touch each briefly with dry hands. They should be close to the same temperature, but the metal usually feels colder because it transfers heat faster. Never test this with very hot or freezing objects.

Try a safe mini experiment

Try it: ask someone to place a metal spoon and a wooden spoon on the same table without telling you which is which. Touch both briefly and note which feels colder. Then discuss how heat moves.

Words made simpleA good experiment changes one thing at a time, observes carefully and compares results. Never taste unknown materials or use dangerous heat, chemicals or electricity.
