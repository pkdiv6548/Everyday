WATER

Why can ice crackle in a drink?

Rapid temperature changes make ice expand and contract unevenly.

4 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerDrop an ice cube into a drink and you may hear sharp little cracks. The sound often comes from stresses inside the ice as different parts warm and change size at different rates.

Temperature changes create stress

Ice is a solid crystal structure. When one part warms faster than another, the parts expand at different rates. Tiny cracks can form or existing cracks can move, releasing energy as a click or crackle. Bubbles and movement in the drink can add other small sounds.

Why is the sound stronger sometimes?

A large temperature difference can create greater internal stress. The shape of the ice, trapped air and the temperature of the drink all affect the sound. Ice from a freezer can be much colder than a drink, so it changes temperature quickly at the surface.

Try a safe mini experiment

With an adult, listen as an ice cube is placed into room-temperature water and then into a cooler drink. Use ordinary drinking glasses on a stable surface and never use hot liquid in a cold glass.

Words made simpleStress is a force spread through a material. A crystal is a solid whose particles form an ordered pattern.
