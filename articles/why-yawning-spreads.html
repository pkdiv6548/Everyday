Why is yawning contagious?

Seeing or thinking about a yawn can trigger the urge in some people.

4 MIN READEVERYDAY ODDITIES
The short answerHave you ever yawned because someone else did? Contagious yawning is common, but scientists still study exactly why it happens and why it varies between people.

The brain notices a familiar action

Yawning is an automatic action involving a wide mouth opening and a deep breath. Seeing, hearing or even reading about yawning can prompt it in some people. Social attention and brain networks involved in observing actions may play a role, but there is no single explanation that covers every case.

Is it always about sleep?

Sleepiness and boredom can be linked to yawning, but people also yawn when changing activity or feeling tension. Popular explanations such as 'the brain needs more oxygen' are not supported as a general explanation. A yawn by itself is not a reliable health test.

Try a safe mini experiment

Notice without forcing it: if you see someone yawn, note whether you yawn too. There is no need to deliberately hold your breath or trigger repeated yawning.

Words made simpleContagious means something can spread from one person or situation to another. An automatic action happens without deliberate planning.
